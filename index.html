Ideal Customer Profile

Use this section to quickly evaluate whether Shopify POS is the right fit before investing in a full scoping conversation.

🎯 The sweet spot
Shopify as the primary commerce platform (not just a frontend) · $500K+ annual retail GMV · 1–50 store locations · Fashion, specialty retail, beauty, food & beverage, electronics, home goods · Migrating from Square, Lightspeed, Clover, Vend, or NetSuite POS · Needs unified online + in-store inventory

✅ Great fit — pitch with confidence

  • ✅Already on Shopify (or migrating) — wants to add in-store
  • ✅Needs unified online + in-store inventory management
  • ✅Migrating from legacy POS: Square, Lightspeed, Clover, Vend
  • ✅Staff turnover is high — needs a simple, quick-to-learn POS
  • ✅1–50 store locations with multi-location inventory sync
  • ✅Shopify Payments available in their country
  • ✅Wants a single system for all channels (D2C + POS + B2B)
  • ✅Fashion, beauty, food & beverage, specialty retail, electronics
  • ✅Needs BOPIS (buy online, pick up in store)
  • ✅Wants tap to pay, gift cards, split payments out of the box

⚠️ Caution — qualify carefully

  • ⚠️Complex real-time pricing per customer (ERP-synced at checkout)
  • ⚠️Advanced promotions engine: tiered loyalty, complex rule stacks
  • ⚠️Needs native inventory transfers, POs, replenishment (requires apps or custom build)
  • ⚠️Offline-first requirement — POS requires stable internet connectivity
  • ⚠️50+ locations with enterprise reporting and advanced back-office
  • ⚠️Institutional payment methods (financial aid, government tenders)
  • ⚠️Operated in China (not currently supported)
  • ⚠️Heavy ERP-first workflow (LS Central, Oracle X-Store model)
  • ⚠️Needs custom fiscal/tax device integrations (some EU markets)
  • ⚠️Wants Android-first hardware (supported, but iOS is more tested)

The 10 Rules for a Smooth Launch

Field-tested guidance from real partner launches, adapted from the EMEA POS Bootcamp 2026 launch playbook. Short on time? These ten rules carry most of the value.

The one rule everything rests on: if a POS device is being set up, paired, or logged in for the first time on opening morning, the launch is already late. Set up and label hardware at HQ, test it in the real store and location, and let store staff simply unbox, plug in, and sell.
1

Set up at HQ, test for real

Build, pair, and label every kit at HQ, then test it on the production store and the location it will run. There's no sandbox for POS hardware.

2

Name every system and its owner

ERP, CRM, IMS, OMS, tax, payment gateway, online store, legacy POS. Anything left unnamed becomes a surprise in launch week.

3

Payments before readers

Confirm the POS payments country and verify Shopify Payments before shipping a single reader. Without it there's no unified customer view or consolidated reporting.

4

A designated admin for device setup

The first login on a new or reset device needs the store owner or a user with the Set up new or updated POS devices permission. A collaborator account is not POS access.

5

One version everywhere

One POS app version, one Hub firmware level, one major OS version pinned per wave and recorded per device.

6

Test → Pilot → Waves

Named waves, each with its own go/no-go. A messy wave slows down the next one. Never during BFCM or a peak period.

7

Train on a proven POS

Train the trainer plus one or two super users per store, 2–4 weeks ahead, on hardware that has already passed a real sale.

8

Fix the store network first

If it works on a mobile hotspot and fails on store Wi-Fi, it's the store network. Wire the counter and plan redundancy where offline isn't an option.

9

Replace, don't debug

Bring a spare bag. It's usually a cable or a reader. Never spend 40 minutes debugging in front of a queue.

10

Cover the whole first week

Be on site the day before. Open on a quiet Tuesday morning, never a Friday or BFCM. Named coverage for week one, then monthly KPI reviews.

Bonus rule — let AI do the heavy lifting, not the judgment. Use Sidekick for reports, data checks, and drafts. Keep go/no-go, scope, and store-day decisions with named people.

Opening-Day Checklist (print one per store)

If a "must be true now" line is still open when customers arrive, you're still setting up hardware, not launching.

✅ Must be true now

  • Hardware set up at HQ, labeled, charged, on the wave's app and firmware version
  • Live test sale already completed at this location
  • Day-before setup done (or this is the day before, not opening day)
  • Spare bag on site: reader, Hub cable, power supply, paper, hotspot, adapter
  • Someone who can set up POS devices is available
  • Legacy POS off the counter (if migrating)
  • Staff trained on a proven POS, super user on shift
  • Contingency plan known if the store can't operate

🚪 Before opening the doors

  • Power → network → open POS. Swap from the spare bag if a device fails
  • Don't re-pair unless something broke
  • The location on screen is this store
  • Search, scan an item from the shelf, test printer, reader, and cash drawer
  • Small card sale + refund + receipt
  • Staff PIN works on every device
  • Previous cash session closed, new one started with float
  • On-site owner and escalation path on a card by the POS

👀 During the first hours

  • Watch the first 10 live sales: contactless, chip, a return if one comes up
  • Orders land in the admin under the right location
  • No extension or app freeze after 30+ minutes of continuous use
  • Staff know who to call

🌙 First-day close

  • Cash session closed and reconciled
  • Issues logged while fresh: what, device ID, time
  • Spares used today logged for restock
  • Payments / ERP / inventory check if in scope (Sidekick: today's sales by location)

🧯 If something fails

  • Swap from the spare bag. Don't debug for 40 minutes in front of a queue
  • Works on hotspot, not store Wi-Fi → store network → merchant IT
  • New or reset device → POS device setup in admin, then location, then wait for sync
  • Hub/firmware issue → check the POS app version first
  • Prepaid card declined (code 62) → merchant contacts the issuer
  • Platform or payments issue → Shopify Support case opened by someone who can authenticate

New in Fall '26 for POS Extensions

🖨️ Printing API

Discover connected hardware printers and print documents from your extension, through a selected printer or the system print dialog.

🌙 Background Extensions

The app-background target runs when POS is ready and stays active for the whole session. Listen for POS events (like completed transactions or cart changes) and run non-UI logic.

💾 Storage API (2026-10 RC)

Persistent local storage on the POS device, isolated to your extension, that survives sessions, restarts, and target changes. Currently in the 2026-10 release candidate.

Core APIs for POS Integrations

⚡ Admin API (GraphQL)

Primary integration layer. Products, inventory, orders, customers, draft orders, locations. Required for any ERP or backend integration.

📝 Draft Orders API

Create pre-built orders from your app, retrieve them in POS, and process native checkout — including partial payments. POS Pro required.

🔔 Webhooks

Event-driven integrations. Key topics: orders/updated, inventory_levels/update, products/update. Use financial_status to track payment lifecycle.

🗂️ Metafields & Custom Data

Extend the data model for products, customers, orders, and locations. Store POS-specific attributes, warranty data, repair notes, etc.

📦 Inventory API

Multi-location inventory management. Sync stock levels, adjust quantities, and manage inventory across all store locations.

📬 Fulfillment Orders API

For BOPIS, ship-from-store, and click-and-collect flows. Required for custom fulfillment routing and 3PL integrations.

🚚 Receiving External Transfers in POS

New guide for ERP → store replenishment: the ERP creates the transfer and shipment via the Admin API, and staff receive it in POS. Requires API 2026-10+ and idempotency keys. POS receives quantities only; rejects and cancellations happen in the admin or API.

🧾 Purchase Orders API

New in Fall '26: read purchase orders through the Admin API (inventoryPurchaseOrders) to sync supplier orders with an ERP or replenishment tool.
🧩 ERP → POS transfer pattern: create with inventoryTransferCreateAsReadyToShip → ship with inventoryShipmentCreateInTransit → store receives in POS (or via inventoryShipmentReceive). Listen to inventory_shipments/receive_items and inventory_transfers/updated to sync received quantities back to the ERP. A companion guide for stock counts is planned once that feature is GA.
⚠️ Known gap — Partial Payments API: There is currently no API for creating or recording partial payments programmatically. The recommended pattern is: app creates a draft order via API → staff retrieves it in POS → processes partial payment natively → track via orders/updated webhook with financial_status: partially_paid → paid.

Run a Compelling POS Demo

A field-tested playbook for partner demos. Built on top of agency feedback (Mathilde Vermeulen, May 2026) plus the latest v11.5 capabilities. Use it as your default flow when pitching POS to a retail merchant.

Demo Prep

🖥️ Hardware mirroring (virtual demos)

  • Simplest method — USB + QuickTime: connect iPad, open QuickTime → New Movie Recording → select iPad as source.
  • AirPlay to Mac works on macOS Monterey+, but USB is more reliable for screen sharing.
  • Once mirrored, share the QuickTime window in Google Meet / Zoom.

🛡️ Backup & resilience

  • Pre-load a backup dev store before the demo for connectivity recovery.
  • Offline resilience demo: POS accepts cash + manual payments offline (manual discounts still work).
  • If offline payments are activated, card payments can be authorized offline and captured when reconnected.
  • Caveat to flag: returns, exchanges, gift card validation, and auto discounts require connection.

🏪 In-person demos

  • Bring current Shopify POS hardware — the primary story is the Shopify POS Hub (central hub for card readers, scanners, receipt printers).
  • Setup is available directly from the POS app — show this live if you can.
  • For click-through demos with no hardware, use the public click-through links (replaces the deprecated SalesLoft library).

Demo Philosophy — Two Pillars

Every retail merchant needs to walk away believing two things: that POS is easy to use, and that it's flexible enough to fit how they actually sell. Frame every element of your demo against these two pillars.

⚡
Pillar 1 · Easy to use

Low effort to train staff

POS v11 (Feb 2026) redesigned cart and checkout for speed — modifiers in a side panel, fewer taps for key actions, multi-line editing. Keyboard shortcuts let staff search, browse, and check out faster (great for high-volume environments). The POS Editor lets merchants configure Smart Grid tiles directly from the app — including preloading discount codes onto tiles. No developer required.

🧩
Pillar 2 · Flexible

Fits how they actually sell

Agencies can build custom POS UI extensions surfaced as Smart Grid tiles — loyalty, zero-party data capture, eligibility lookups, dept billing. UI extensions sit next to discounts, products, and collections on the Smart Grid — strong agency-value angle. Modern hardware supports chip/pin, tap to pay, cash, and contactless across 100+ countries.

The 8-Element Demo Flow

1

Cart Building & Checkout

Create an order, search products (highlight word completion + matched-variant display in results), check out.

  • Emphasize: products, pricing, promotions, and tax handled by Shopify — no manual data entry, no reconciliation.
  • Show keyboard shortcuts and the redesigned v11 side-panel modifiers.
2

Flexible Fulfillment — Show All Four Modes

The four-mode story is what differentiates Shopify POS in the unified-commerce conversation.

  • BOPIS (Buy Online, Pickup In-Store): staff create same-store pickup orders from the POS cart (v11.3). Customer notifications fire automatically.
  • Buy In-Store, Ship to Home: item out at this location but in stock elsewhere — offer to ship home, waive shipping, add a discount, complete one checkout covering both.
  • Browse In-Store, Buy Online (Email Cart): customer isn't ready — staff emails the cart so they can complete online. Captures sales that would otherwise walk out.
  • Local delivery: configured under Fulfillment locations alongside pickup, and as of Fall '26 staff can create local delivery orders right from POS. The four modes together make the strongest flexible-fulfillment story.
3

Inventory Transfers Between Locations

Show outgoing transfers from the POS app (Orders → Outgoing transfers). Staff can pick, pack, and send inventory without going to Admin.

  • Barcode-driven workflow: staff scan items using the iPad's built-in camera or an external scanner during pick/pack and receiving — no separate handheld device required. Strong differentiator vs. legacy POS systems.
  • Inventory is decremented live and visible across all locations instantly.
  • Note to flag: transfers are created in Admin or by an ERP via the Admin API (see Receiving External Transfers); POS supports fulfilling and receiving only. Requires POS Pro.
4

Returns & Exchanges (v11.5, April 2026)

Returns were redesigned in v11.5 — they now flow through the cart, the same flow used for sales.

  • Show a return and demonstrate the new Split Refund screen for choosing refund destinations (original payment, gift card, store credit, split).
  • Capture a return reason. Data flows to Admin and connects to apps like Klaviyo through one integration, not two.
  • Caveats to flag: returns and exchanges require internet connection — they cannot be processed offline. Exchanges require POS Pro.
5

Discount Codes & Split Tender

Staff can create and scan discount codes directly in POS. Show a Smart Grid discount tile preloaded with a code — one tap at the register.

  • Strong talking point for promotional events and discount accountability.
  • Pair with split-tender to demonstrate flexibility (e.g., gift card + card).
6

Store Fulfillment (Ship-From-Store)

Show the associate fulfillment flow — viewing open orders, picking and packing with barcode scanning, shipping from store inventory.

  • Inventory is decremented live and visible across all locations.
  • Reinforces the unified-commerce pitch: every store is a fulfillment node.
7

Agency-Built UI Extension

Build a simple custom UI extension and add it as a Smart Grid tile. Keep it intentionally simple — the goal is showing what's possible and that you can extend the platform to fit the merchant's specific workflow.

  • UI extensions sit next to discounts, products, and collections on the Smart Grid.
  • Common starter patterns: loyalty lookup, customer profile, eligibility check, dept billing, gift card balance.
8

Reporting in Admin

Close the loop in Admin: show online vs. in-store sales side by side. Pull a specific product's performance across channels — units sold, revenue per channel.

  • In-store sales are attributed by location — full visibility for multi-location merchants.
  • One source of truth for finance, merchandising, and ops.
